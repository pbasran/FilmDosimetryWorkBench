Signal definition

xc = (c − B) / max, for c = R, G  ·  dose = A / (xc + C) + B

Subtracting the blue channel removes the part of the signal that does not track dose (thickness and scanner non-uniformity), as in the lab's MATLAB routine.

R, G, B are 8-bit-equivalent means. Δ is fitted dose minus entered dose, in Gy. Points without a dose are ignored by the fit. A control is an unirradiated film scanned with the batch; it enters the fit as 0 Gy. Its ROI can be moved by clicking in its image.

Straighten and crop

Open a film scan.

Auto finds the film against the scanner background and levels its outline, then crops to it. The film's edges need not be perpendicular to the beam: for a manual fix type a rotation, use Level edge to drag along any edge or a field edge, then draw the Export region. Rotation resamples the scan (bilinear); the original file is unchanged.

Conversion

Channel weightsred 0% · green 100%

Each n × n block of pixels is averaged per channel, converted to a net signal, then to dose with the fitted curve. Dose is never negative and is clipped at the limit above. The limit follows 1.2 × your highest calibration dose (rounded up to a multiple of 5 Gy) until you change it.

Export

Convert a film first.

The 32-bit TIFF stores dose in Gy directly. The 16-bit TIFF and the DICOM store integers with a scale factor (written into the file). DICOM export is for research use: the film plane is mapped to the patient X–Y plane at the origin, and the dose is not tied to a treatment plan.

Drag on any of the four maps to draw the ROI (while this mode is on, dragging draws the ROI; use the slider to move the profile). Coordinates are mm from the film centre, in the film image.

Plate geometry (Corning 48-well)

Wells A–F run top to bottom and 1–8 left to right in the film image. The analysed area of each well is a circle 1 mm inside the well wall (edit the inset above). Drag on any map to move the plate, or type the centre. Geometry defaults are the lab's RADAR plate values (pitch 13.08 mm, bottom diameter 11.05 mm). Set the threshold to 0 to include unirradiated wells in the global statistics; the per-well table ignores the threshold.

Normalization and threshold

Each distribution is divided by its own value (100 %). The threshold is applied to the RTDOSE, so the same pixels are used for both. With no normalization the threshold is a fraction of the RTDOSE maximum in the film area. For the point option, click on the Film or RTDOSE map.
